# Negotiation

## BATNA: Best Alternative to Negotiated Agreement

Your BATNA is your walkaway power. The stronger it is, the more confidently
you can negotiate.

**How to determine your BATNA:**
- List all alternatives if this negotiation fails
- Evaluate each alternative realistically (not optimistically)
- Select the best one -- that's your BATNA
- Never reveal your BATNA unless it strengthens your position

**Using BATNA in practice:**
- If the offer is worse than your BATNA, walk away
- If the offer is better than your BATNA, you have a deal (but push for more)
- Improve your BATNA before negotiating (get competing offers)
- Estimate the other party's BATNA -- it reveals their flexibility

**Common mistakes:**
- Negotiating without a BATNA (you'll accept anything)
- Confusing BATNA with your ideal outcome
- Revealing desperation ("I really need this job")
- Not investing time to create alternatives

## Salary Negotiation Framework

**1. Research (before the conversation):**
- Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Blind, recruiter friends
- Target: 75th percentile for role + location + company tier
- Know the full package: base, bonus, equity, benefits, PTO
- Have a specific number (ranges invite the bottom)

**2. Anchor high:**
- State your number first (anchoring effect is real)
- "Based on my research and experience, I'm targeting $X"
- Anchor 10-20% above your actual target
- Never give a range -- they'll hear the bottom

**3. Justify with evidence:**
- "Here's why I'm worth $X" -- not "here's what I need"
- Cite competing offers, market data, unique skills
- Reference specific impact at previous roles with numbers
- Frame as mutual value: "Here's what I'll deliver at this level"

**4. Use silence:**
- After stating your number, stop talking
- Discomfort drives concessions
- Let them respond first
- "I'd like to think about that" buys time without conceding

**5. Negotiate non-salary levers:**
- Signing bonus (one-time, easier to approve)
- Equity refresh/grant
- Title (affects future negotiations)
- Remote work, PTO, learning budget
- Start date, review timeline, promotion path

**Never:**
- Disclose current salary (illegal to ask in many jurisdictions)
- Accept on the spot ("I'm excited -- let me review the full package")
- Negotiate against yourself
- Threaten without willingness to follow through

## Promotion Negotiation

**Preparation:**
- Document 6-12 months of staff-level work (brag doc)
- Know the leveling criteria for your target level
- Map your work to each criterion explicitly
- Identify your sponsor (director+ who will advocate)

**The conversation:**
- "I'd like to discuss my path to [level]. Here's what I've been delivering..."
- Present evidence, not aspiration
- Ask: "What gaps do you see?" (forces specifics)
- Get a timeline: "What would you need to see in the next quarter?"

**If blocked:**
- Ask for specific, measurable criteria
- "What would it take?" -- get it in writing
- Set a checkpoint: "Let's revisit in 3 months with these criteria met"
- If criteria keep shifting, that's a signal to explore externally

**Nuclear option:**
- External offer forces a decision (but burns trust if bluffing)
- Only use if genuinely willing to leave
- Frame carefully: "I've been approached and want to be transparent"

## Scope Negotiation with Stakeholders

When someone wants you to take on more work without more resources or time.

**Step 1: Acknowledge the ask**
- "I understand this is important because..."
- Show you heard them before pushing back

**Step 2: Make the tradeoff visible**
- "If we add X, we need to drop or delay Y"
- "We can do X in this timeline with these cuts to scope"
- Use data: "Our current velocity is N points/sprint"
- Never say "we can't" -- say "here's what it costs"

**Step 3: Offer options**
- Option A: Full scope, extended timeline
- Option B: Reduced scope, original timeline
- Option C: Full scope, original timeline, more people
- Let them choose. You've reframed from "yes/no" to "which tradeoff"

**Step 4: Get commitment**
- "So we're agreed on Option B? I'll update the roadmap."
- Follow up in writing (email/doc) to prevent scope creep
- Reference this agreement when new asks arrive

## When and How to Push Back

**Push back when:**
- The ask conflicts with agreed priorities
- Quality or security would be compromised
- The timeline is unrealistic and failure is predictable
- You have data the requester doesn't

**How to push back effectively:**
- Lead with shared goals: "We both want X to succeed..."
- Use data over opinion: "The last three times we rushed, we had incidents"
- Propose alternatives, don't just say no
- Escalate early if you can't resolve it peer-to-peer
- Pick your battles -- push back on things that matter

**Phrases that work:**
- "Help me understand the urgency"
- "What would you deprioritize to make room?"
- "I want to make sure we're not setting ourselves up to fail"
- "I'm concerned about X because of Y -- can we discuss?"

**Anti-patterns:**
- Saying yes and then missing the deadline
- Passive resistance (agreeing but not executing)
- Escalating everything (loses credibility)
- Making it personal ("you always dump work on us")
