# Staff Engineer Path

## What Staff Engineers Actually Do

Staff engineers operate as force multipliers. They don't just write code -- they
set technical direction, unblock teams, and ensure the right problems get solved.

Core responsibilities:
- Define technical strategy for a domain or org
- Make decisions that cross team boundaries
- Mentor senior engineers toward independence
- Write and review RFCs/design docs at the org level
- Identify systemic problems before they become fires
- Build alignment between engineering and business goals

The job is roughly 30% writing code, 30% writing docs, 40% communication.

## Staff Archetypes

**Tech Lead** -- Partners with a single team's EM. Owns technical roadmap,
unblocks engineers, reviews designs. Deepest involvement in day-to-day delivery.

**Architect** -- Owns cross-team technical direction for a domain. Focuses on
system boundaries, API contracts, and long-term evolution. Less hands-on coding.

**Solver** -- Parachutes into the hardest problems across the org. Deep expertise
in specific areas. Often works alone or in small groups on critical projects.

**Right Hand** -- Extension of a senior leader (VP/CTO). Represents them in
rooms, drives org-wide initiatives, handles the "important but not urgent" work
that leaders can't get to.

Most staff engineers blend archetypes. The mix shifts based on org needs.

## Getting Promoted to Staff

Promotion to staff requires demonstrating you already operate at that level.

**Visibility patterns:**
- Write RFCs that get adopted across teams
- Present at architecture reviews and all-hands
- Be the person others seek out for hard problems
- Ship a project that visibly changes the company's trajectory

**Impact requirements:**
- Multi-team or org-wide scope
- Sustained impact over 6-12 months (not one heroic week)
- Evidence of judgment: knowing what NOT to build

**Sponsorship:**
- You need a sponsor (director+) who will advocate in promotion committees
- Build this relationship through shared projects and trust
- Make your sponsor's job easy: give them concrete evidence

## Writing Promotion Documents

**Brag doc structure (maintain continuously):**
- Project name, dates, your specific contribution
- Quantified impact (latency reduced 40%, saved $X/month)
- Scope indicators (number of teams involved, systems touched)
- Evidence of staff-level judgment (tradeoffs made, alternatives rejected)

**Promotion packet tips:**
- Lead with the strongest 3-4 projects, not an exhaustive list
- Map each project to a staff-level competency
- Include peer quotes that demonstrate cross-team influence
- Show a trajectory: increasing scope over time

## Scope Expansion Patterns

How to organically grow toward staff scope:

1. **Volunteer for cross-team work** -- Migrations, platform improvements, shared
   tooling. These naturally require staff-level coordination.

2. **Write the doc nobody asked for** -- Architectural vision, tech debt strategy,
   onboarding guide for your domain. This demonstrates ownership.

3. **Become the bridge** -- Connect teams that should be talking but aren't.
   Identify duplicated efforts and propose consolidation.

4. **Turn tribal knowledge into systems** -- Runbooks, automation, guardrails.
   This scales your impact beyond your own hands.

5. **Take the meeting** -- Represent engineering in cross-functional discussions.
   Product reviews, incident retrospectives, vendor evaluations.

Anti-patterns to avoid:
- Scope-grabbing without delivering on current commitments
- Optimizing for visibility over actual impact
- Skipping the "boring" work (on-call, code review, mentoring)
- Waiting to be asked instead of proposing

## Timeline Expectations

Senior to Staff typically takes 2-4 years of sustained staff-level work.
Companies with fewer staff titles (no "senior staff") may take longer.
Switching companies can accelerate or reset the clock depending on leveling.

The fastest path: find a critical problem nobody owns, solve it visibly,
and make sure leadership knows the impact.
