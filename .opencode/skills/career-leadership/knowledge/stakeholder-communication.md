# Stakeholder Communication

## Executive Communication (BLUF)

### Bottom Line Up Front
- Lead with the conclusion or ask
- Then provide supporting evidence
- End with next steps or decision needed
- Executives scan -- put critical info in first sentence

### Format for Execs
```
BLUF: [What you need them to know or decide]
Context: [2-3 sentences of relevant background]
Options: [If decision needed, list 2-3 with tradeoffs]
Recommendation: [Your suggested path]
Ask: [What you need from them specifically]
```

### Common Mistakes
- Burying the ask at the bottom
- Too much technical detail (they don't need implementation)
- No clear call to action
- Presenting problems without proposed solutions

## Status Updates That Get Read

### RAG Format
- **Red**: Blocked, needs escalation, timeline at risk
- **Amber**: Risk identified, mitigation in progress
- **Green**: On track, no intervention needed

### One-Pager Template
```
Project: [Name]
Status: [GREEN/AMBER/RED]
Progress: [What shipped since last update]
Next: [What ships next week]
Risks: [What could go wrong, what you're doing about it]
Needs: [Decisions or resources needed]
```

### Tips
- Send at the same time weekly (build habit)
- Keep to 5-7 bullet points max
- Link to details rather than including them
- Call out changes from last week explicitly

## Influence Without Authority

### Building Credibility
- Ship things that work -- track record is currency
- Share knowledge freely (tech talks, docs, reviews)
- Follow through on every commitment, no matter how small
- Admit mistakes early -- credibility grows from honesty

### Using Data
- Frame proposals with numbers, not opinions
- Show before/after (latency, error rate, developer time)
- Benchmark against industry standards
- Let data speak -- don't editorialize excessively

### Storytelling
- Connect technical work to business outcomes
- Use concrete examples, not abstract principles
- "Last month we had 3 incidents because of X" > "X is risky"
- Paint the future state vividly

## Managing Up

### Anticipate Needs
- Know your manager's priorities and pressures
- Surface problems before they surface themselves
- Bring options, not just questions
- Keep a running doc of wins and decisions for 1:1s

### Frame Options
- Present 2-3 paths with tradeoffs
- State your recommendation and reasoning
- Include what you'd need for each option
- Make it easy to say yes

### Own Decisions
- Once aligned, execute without re-asking
- Document decisions so you can reference them later
- Update stakeholders on outcomes (closes the loop)
- Take responsibility for outcomes, good or bad

## Cross-Functional Collaboration

### Working with Product
- Ask "what problem are we solving?" before "what should we build?"
- Push back on scope with data, not frustration
- Propose phased delivery (MVP, fast follow, future)
- Share technical constraints early -- they inform prioritization

### Working with Design
- Engage early in the design process (feasibility input)
- Ask about edge cases and error states
- Provide feedback on interaction cost, not aesthetics
- Respect the craft -- designers solve real problems

### Working with Other Teams
- Define interfaces and contracts explicitly
- Over-communicate timelines and dependencies
- Use shared documents, not chat threads, for decisions
- Thank people publicly when they unblock you

## Difficult Conversations Framework

### Preparation
- Write down your intent (what outcome do you want?)
- Separate observation from interpretation
- Anticipate their perspective and concerns
- Choose timing and setting deliberately

### During the Conversation
- State observations: "I noticed X happened"
- Share impact: "The effect was Y"
- Ask their view: "What's your perspective?"
- Propose path forward: "Going forward, I'd suggest Z"

### Common Scenarios
- Giving feedback to a peer (focus on behavior, not character)
- Disagreeing with your manager (frame as shared goals)
- Addressing underperformance (specific examples, clear ask)
- Saying no to requests (explain tradeoff, offer alternatives)

### After
- Summarize agreements in writing
- Follow up on commitments from both sides
- Don't hold grudges -- move forward
- Check in later to see if things improved
