# Interview Preparation (2026)

**New reality:** AI handles leetcode-style problems, so companies test differently. Rote algorithm grinding is no longer the differentiator. Interviews now probe judgment, decomposition, and how you work WITH AI.

## What's Tested

- **System design (~45 min):** the highest-signal round for senior+.
- **Behavioral (~30 min):** leadership, conflict, ambiguity, impact.
- **Coding with AI awareness:** pair problem-solving, reviewing and correcting AI output, not blank-page recall.
- **Architecture deep-dive:** walk through a system you built, defend tradeoffs.
- **Cultural / values fit:** collaboration, ownership, communication.

## System Design Prep

Framework, in order:
1. **Requirements** - functional + non-functional, clarify scale, constraints.
2. **API** - define the contract before the boxes.
3. **High-level design (HLD)** - components, data flow, storage choices.
4. **Deep dive** - pick 1-2 areas, go deep (scaling, consistency, failure modes).

- Practice ~10 canonical problems (URL shortener, feed, chat, rate limiter, etc.).
- Always explain TRADEOFFS out loud: why this DB, why this consistency model.
- Talk through failure and scaling as a first-class concern, not an afterthought.

## Behavioral Prep

Use **STAR**: Situation, Task, Action, Result.
- Prepare 5-7 stories covering: conflict, failure, leadership, a hard technical decision, and navigating ambiguity.
- Quantify results ("cut p99 latency 40%", "unblocked 3 teams").
- One strong story can flex to answer several prompts.

## Coding Rounds

- Less about syntax recall, more about problem decomposition.
- Show testing strategy and edge-case thinking explicitly.
- If AI tooling is allowed, demonstrate you can review, verify, and correct its output, not just accept it.

## Timeline & Resources

- **4-8 weeks** of serious, consistent prep for a strong loop.
- Do mock interviews with peers; being watched changes your performance.
- Resources: system design - ByteByteGo, *Designing Data-Intensive Applications* (DDIA); behavioral - Gergely Orosz (The Pragmatic Engineer) guides.
