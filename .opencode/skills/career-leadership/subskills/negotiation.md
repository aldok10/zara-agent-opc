# Compensation Negotiation

Negotiating is expected, not rude. The offer is a starting point. Failing to counter leaves money on the table every year via compounding raises.

## Research First

- Know your market rate before any conversation.
- Sources: levels.fyi (most reliable for tech), Glassdoor, Blind.
- Anchor to level + location + company tier, not a single anecdote.

## Never Give a Number First

When asked for salary expectations:
> "I'd like to understand the full scope and level of the role first before discussing numbers."

Let them anchor. The first number often reveals their band.

## Counter-Offer Framework

1. **Express enthusiasm** - reaffirm you want to join. Negotiation is collaborative, not adversarial.
2. **Present data** - market rate, any competing offer, your unique value.
3. **Propose a specific number** - 10-20% above the initial offer. Specific beats "more".
4. **Be silent after proposing** - resist filling the pause. Let them respond.

## Components to Negotiate

- Base salary
- Equity (RSU vesting schedule, refresh grants, not just the headline grant)
- Signing bonus (easiest lever; offsets unvested equity you leave behind)
- Level / title (affects all future comp and scope)
- Remote / location flexibility
- PTO
- Learning / conference budget

## When to Walk Away

- Below market with no credible path up.
- Toxic signals during the process (disrespect, chaos, evasiveness).
- Role misaligned with your growth direction.

## Timing & Leverage

- Negotiate AFTER the offer, never during interviews.
- Multiple competing offers = your strongest position; create optionality by interviewing in batches.
- Get everything in writing before accepting.

Stay polite but firm throughout. A well-run negotiation signals seniority, not greed.
