# Engineering Visibility & Personal Brand

**"Your best work is worthless if nobody knows about it."** Visibility isn't self-promotion for its own sake, it's making your impact legible to the people who decide your scope, promotion, and next opportunity.

## Why It Matters

- Promotions are decided by people who didn't watch you do the work.
- Managers forget; documentation of impact fills the gap.
- Influence at staff+ requires being known across team boundaries.

## Internal Visibility

- Write internal blog posts / wiki entries about what you built and why.
- Present at team demos and eng all-hands.
- Volunteer for cross-team initiatives where more people see your work.
- Write RFCs that get adopted; a widely-referenced doc is durable influence.

## External Visibility

- Conference talks - start local and small (meetups), then scale up.
- Blog posts - aim for 1-2 per month; consistency compounds.
- Open source contributions - visible, verifiable proof of skill.
- Technical content on Twitter/X and LinkedIn - share what you learn.

## Brag Document

A weekly log of your impact. This is the single highest-leverage habit here.
- **Format:** what I did, who it helped, what metric moved.
- Log weekly (5 minutes), compile monthly.
- **Use for:** performance reviews, promotion packets, resume updates, 1:1s.
- Without it, you'll under-report your own year at review time.

## Portfolio

- GitHub profile that reflects real work.
- Personal site as a hub for talks, posts, projects.
- Published articles you can point to.

## Anti-Patterns

- Self-promotion without substance (hype with no results behind it).
- Quantity over quality (10 shallow posts < 2 deep ones).
- Comparing yourself to others (survivorship bias; you see their highlight reel).
