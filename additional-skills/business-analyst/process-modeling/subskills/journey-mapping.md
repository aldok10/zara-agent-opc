# Journey Mapping

Touchpoints, pain points, opportunities, and emotions in customer experience documentation.

## When to Load

- Mapping end-to-end customer experiences
- Identifying pain points and improvement opportunities
- Aligning teams around customer perspective
- Prioritizing feature work based on user impact

## Key Concepts

**Touchpoints** -- Every interaction between user and product/service. Digital (website, app, email) and physical (store, phone, mail). Map chronologically across the experience.

**Pain Points** -- Moments of friction, confusion, or frustration. Rate severity (minor annoyance vs deal-breaker). Link to quantitative data (drop-off rates, support tickets).

**Opportunities** -- Gaps between current and ideal experience. Classify: quick wins (low effort, high impact), strategic investments (high effort, high impact), nice-to-haves.

**Emotional Arc** -- User's emotional state through the journey. Positive peaks (delight moments) and negative valleys (frustration). Design to end on a high note (peak-end rule).

**Stages** -- Group touchpoints into phases: Awareness, Consideration, Purchase, Onboarding, Usage, Renewal/Advocacy. Stages vary by business model.

## Gotchas

- Journey maps based on assumptions instead of research are fiction -- validate with real user data
- Too many personas dilute focus -- start with 1-2 primary personas
- Mapping only the happy path misses the painful moments that drive churn
- Journey maps without assigned owners for each pain point become shelf-ware
- Forgetting post-purchase stages (support, renewal, advocacy) misses retention opportunities

## Verification

- Based on real user research (interviews, analytics, support data) -- not assumptions
- Covers the complete journey including failure/recovery paths
- Pain points are linked to measurable data (ticket volume, drop-off rate, NPS scores)
- Opportunities are prioritized by impact and effort
- Stakeholders from each touchpoint have reviewed and agreed on the map
