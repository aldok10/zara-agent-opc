# Elicitation

## Core Concepts

- Requirements don't exist pre-formed — they must be drawn out through structured inquiry
- Stakeholders describe solutions, not problems; your job is to uncover the underlying need
- Multiple techniques needed: interviews alone miss what observation and data analysis reveal
- Context matters: who uses the system, where, under what constraints, with what workarounds
- Silence and contradiction are signals — pursue both

## Key Practices

- Ask "why" at least three times to move from stated solution to actual problem
- Use concrete scenarios: "Walk me through last Tuesday when this went wrong"
- Observe actual workflows before accepting described workflows — people omit habits
- Cross-reference answers between stakeholders to find contradictions early
- Document assumptions explicitly and validate them — never leave them implicit

## Common Mistakes

- Accepting the first answer as the real requirement without probing deeper
- Only interviewing managers while ignoring the people who do the actual work
- Conflating "what the system should do" with "how it should do it"

## When to Apply

- Starting any new initiative or feature discovery
- Requirements feel vague, contradictory, or incomplete
- Building for users you haven't directly observed
- Stakeholders disagree on what the system should do
