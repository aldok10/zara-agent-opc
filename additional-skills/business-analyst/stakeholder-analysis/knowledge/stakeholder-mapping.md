# Stakeholder Mapping

## Power/Interest Matrix

The primary tool for categorizing stakeholders based on their ability to influence the project and their level of interest in it.

```
              High Power
                 |
    Manage       |     Collaborate
    Closely      |     (Key Players)
                 |
  ---------------+---------------
                 |
    Monitor      |     Keep
    (Minimal)    |     Informed
                 |
              Low Power

   Low Interest         High Interest
```

### Quadrant Strategies

| Quadrant | Power | Interest | Strategy |
|----------|-------|----------|----------|
| Key Players | High | High | Collaborate: involve in decisions, regular 1:1s |
| Manage Closely | High | Low | Keep satisfied: don't overwhelm with detail, highlight impact |
| Keep Informed | Low | High | Consult: provide updates, gather feedback, manage expectations |
| Monitor | Low | Low | Minimal effort: periodic updates, available if needed |

## Influence vs. Impact Matrix

Alternative framework focusing on who can influence outcomes vs. who is impacted by them.

- **High influence, high impact**: Decision makers who are also affected
- **High influence, low impact**: Sponsors, executives (approve but don't use)
- **Low influence, high impact**: End users (affected but can't change direction)
- **Low influence, low impact**: Peripheral stakeholders

## Identifying Stakeholders

### Categories to Consider

- **Sponsors**: Who funds this? Who approves budget?
- **Decision makers**: Who has final say on scope, timeline, features?
- **Users**: Who will use the system daily? Occasionally?
- **Affected parties**: Whose workflow changes even if they don't use the system?
- **Technical**: Who builds, maintains, operates?
- **Regulatory**: Who enforces compliance?
- **Support**: Who handles issues after launch?
- **Partners**: External parties who integrate or depend on this?

### Discovery Questions

- Who gains from this project succeeding?
- Who loses if this project succeeds? (resistance sources)
- Whose approval is needed to proceed?
- Who controls resources (budget, people, systems)?
- Who has veto power?
- Who will be blamed if it fails?
- Who has done this before and has institutional knowledge?

## Engagement Levels

| Level | Description | Signs |
|-------|-------------|-------|
| Unaware | Doesn't know about the project | No communication received |
| Resistant | Aware but opposed | Raises objections, delays, avoids |
| Neutral | Aware but indifferent | Attends if asked, no initiative |
| Supportive | Positive, willing to help | Provides input, advocates |
| Champion | Actively drives success | Removes blockers, recruits others |

### Moving Stakeholders

To move from Resistant to Neutral:
- Understand their concerns (listen first)
- Address legitimate objections
- Show "what's in it for them"
- Give them influence over relevant decisions

To move from Neutral to Supportive:
- Share early wins
- Make it easy to participate
- Recognize their contributions
- Connect project goals to their goals

To move from Supportive to Champion:
- Give them ownership of visible outcomes
- Provide information they can share with their network
- Acknowledge their advocacy publicly

## Stakeholder Register

Document for each stakeholder:

```
| Name | Role | Power | Interest | Current Engagement | Target Engagement | Strategy | Owner |
```

### Maintenance

- Review monthly (stakeholders change roles, lose interest, gain power)
- Update when org changes happen
- Re-assess after major project milestones
- Pay attention to new stakeholders entering mid-project

## RACI for Key Decisions

| Decision | Responsible | Accountable | Consulted | Informed |
|----------|-------------|-------------|-----------|----------|

Rules:
- Exactly one Accountable per decision
- Responsible does the work
- Consulted means two-way communication BEFORE decision
- Informed means one-way communication AFTER decision
- Keep Consulted list short (too many = decision paralysis)

## Coalition Building

When facing resistance:
1. Identify your champions (high power, supportive)
2. Map who influences the resistant stakeholders
3. Find common ground (shared goals, even if different motivations)
4. Build a critical mass of support before escalating conflicts
5. Address the loudest resistor last (build momentum first)

## Political Awareness

- Understand reporting lines and informal influence networks
- Know who talks to whom outside meetings
- Identify historical conflicts between stakeholders
- Recognize that stated positions may differ from actual interests
- Watch for proxy conflicts (arguing about features but actually about control)
