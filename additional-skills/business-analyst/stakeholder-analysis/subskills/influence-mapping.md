# Influence Mapping

Formal and informal power structures, coalition building, and navigating organizational dynamics.

## When to Load

- Understanding who really makes decisions (beyond org charts)
- Building support for a proposal or initiative
- Navigating organizational resistance to change
- Identifying allies and potential blockers

## Key Concepts

**Formal Power** -- Authority derived from position: title, reporting lines, budget ownership, signing authority. Visible on org charts. Necessary but not sufficient for influence.

**Informal Power** -- Influence derived from: expertise (technical authority), relationships (trusted advisor), information access, track record, social capital. Often more impactful than formal power.

**Coalition Building** -- Assembling a group of supporters before a decision point. Identify: early adopters, swing votes, and potential blockers. Build momentum through sequential buy-in.

**Influence Flows** -- Who defers to whom? Whose opinion changes minds? Map by observing: who gets consulted before decisions, who can veto without formal authority, who mentors decision-makers.

**Resistance Patterns** -- Active (vocal opposition), passive (delays, non-compliance), political (undermining in private). Each requires different response: address concerns, create urgency, expose to group.

## Patterns

```
# Influence Map Structure

Decision: Adopt microservices architecture

Champions (actively supporting):
  - Sarah (Staff Eng): Technical credibility, wrote the proposal
  - Mike (VP Eng): Formal authority, sponsoring budget

Swing Votes (undecided, persuadable):
  - James (Platform Lead): Concerned about operational complexity
    -> Influence approach: Address ops concerns with concrete runbook
  - Lisa (Product VP): Cares about delivery speed
    -> Influence approach: Show timeline improvement data

Blockers (opposing):
  - Tom (Senior Eng): Prefers monolith, high informal credibility
    -> Influence approach: Involve in design, address legitimate concerns
  - Finance: Budget increase concern
    -> Influence approach: ROI analysis via Mike (their peer)

Influence Paths:
  Sarah -> Tom (peer respect, technical credibility)
  Mike -> Finance (same leadership level, budget context)
  James -> Lisa (cross-functional partner, delivery accountability)
```

```
# Coalition Building Sequence

1. Secure champion (Sarah) -- already aligned
2. Get sponsor commitment (Mike) -- needs ROI framing
3. Address swing votes individually:
   a. James: ops workshop to address concerns (Week 1)
   b. Lisa: delivery timeline analysis (Week 2)
4. Neutralize blockers:
   a. Tom: involve in architecture review, incorporate feedback (Week 2-3)
   b. Finance: Mike presents cost analysis (Week 3)
5. Group decision meeting: majority already aligned (Week 4)
```

## Gotchas

- Mapping only formal authority misses the "kitchen cabinet" advisors who shape decisions
- Attempting to override blockers instead of addressing their concerns creates enemies
- Coalition building that feels manipulative backfires -- be transparent about goals
- Influence maps are point-in-time snapshots -- relationships shift after reorgs, departures
- Ignoring "low power" people who have the ear of decision-makers (EAs, trusted ICs)

## Verification

- Map identifies both formal authority AND informal influence for each person
- Blockers have specific, addressable concerns documented (not just "opposes")
- Coalition sequence is ordered (easy wins first to build momentum)
- Influence approach for each person is tailored to their concerns and motivations
- Map has been updated after organizational changes or new information
