# Performance Management

Review cycles, expectations setting, performance improvement plans, and promotion criteria.

## When to Load

- Running performance review cycles
- Setting clear expectations for engineers
- Writing or executing a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)
- Defining promotion criteria and building cases

## Key Concepts

**Expectations** -- Written, leveled, specific. What does "meeting expectations" look like at this level? Cover: technical delivery, scope, collaboration, mentoring (by level). Share on day one, not review day.

**Review Cycles** -- Quarterly lightweight check-ins + annual/biannual formal reviews. Gather peer feedback (360), self-assessment, manager assessment. No surprises -- reviews confirm what was already discussed in 1:1s.

**Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)** -- Last resort after repeated feedback has not produced change. Time-bound (30-60 days), specific measurable goals, weekly check-ins, clear success/failure criteria. Document everything.

**Promotion** -- Criteria: already performing at the next level consistently (not a reward for current-level excellence). Build a case with concrete evidence. Avoid: time-based promotion, compensation-driven promotion.

**Calibration** -- Cross-team comparison to ensure consistent standards. Same rating should mean the same thing across teams. Prevents grade inflation and inconsistent bars.

## Patterns

```markdown
# Level Expectations: Senior Engineer (L5)

## Technical
- Owns and delivers complex projects (2-3 month scope) independently
- Makes sound technical decisions considering long-term maintenance
- Identifies and addresses technical debt proactively
- Produces code that rarely requires significant revision in review

## Scope
- Leads technical direction for a team-sized domain
- Influences roadmap through technical insight
- Breaks down ambiguous problems into actionable plans

## Collaboration
- Mentors mid-level engineers (visible growth in mentees)
- Communicates technical decisions clearly to non-technical stakeholders
- Resolves disagreements constructively, builds consensus

## Delivery
- Consistently delivers on commitments with minimal re-planning
- Proactively identifies and mitigates risks
- Balances speed with quality appropriately for context
```

```markdown
# PIP Structure

Name: [Engineer]
Duration: 45 days
Start: [Date] | End: [Date]
Weekly check-in: [Day/Time]

## Context
[Brief, factual description of performance gap -- specific examples]

## Goals (must be SMART)
1. [Specific measurable goal with timeline]
2. [Specific measurable goal with timeline]
3. [Specific measurable goal with timeline]

## Support Provided
- Weekly 1:1 focused on progress
- Pairing with [senior engineer] on [specific area]
- Reduced scope to focus on improvement areas

## Success Criteria
Meeting expectations: All 3 goals achieved by end date
Not meeting expectations: Fewer than 3 goals achieved

## Outcomes
- Success: PIP closed, regular performance management resumes
- Failure: Separation discussion with HR
```

## Gotchas

- Surprise negative reviews indicate the manager failed at ongoing feedback, not that the engineer failed
- PIPs used as "documentation for firing" instead of genuine improvement attempts are unethical and legally risky
- Promotion criteria that are not written down lead to bias and inconsistency
- Calibration without clear level definitions becomes a popularity contest
- Rating inflation ("everyone is exceeding") makes the system meaningless

## Verification

- Every engineer has written expectations for their current level (shared, not hidden)
- No review contains feedback that was not previously discussed in 1:1s
- PIP goals are specific and measurable (not "improve communication")
- Promotion cases cite concrete evidence at the next level (not "deserves it" or tenure)
- Calibration results are consistent: same performance = same rating across teams
