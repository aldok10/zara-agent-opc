# OKR Anti-Patterns

## Output vs Outcome

### The Most Common Mistake

**Output (wrong)**: "Launch the new search feature by March"
**Outcome (right)**: "Users find what they need 40% faster (measured by time-to-first-result)"

### Why It Matters
- Outputs measure effort spent, outcomes measure value delivered
- You can launch a feature nobody uses (output achieved, value zero)
- Outcomes allow teams to find the best solution, not just the assigned one
- Pivoting is possible when targeting outcomes, not fixed deliverables

### The Litmus Test
Ask: "If we achieve this KR but customers aren't better off, did we succeed?"
- If yes → it's an output, reframe it
- If no → it's an outcome, keep it

### Reframing Examples
| Output KR | Outcome KR |
|---|---|
| Ship mobile app v2.0 | Mobile user retention increases to 60% |
| Complete 3 hiring sprints | Time-to-fill reduced from 45 to 30 days |
| Migrate to microservices | Deploy frequency increases from weekly to daily |
| Write 10 blog posts | Organic traffic grows 25% |

## Sandbagging

### What It Looks Like
- Teams set KRs they know they can hit (low ambition)
- 100% achievement every quarter (too easy)
- No learning or growth happening

### Root Causes
- Punishment for missing targets (fear-based culture)
- OKRs tied directly to compensation/bonuses
- Public shaming of teams that miss targets
- No distinction between committed and aspirational

### Solutions
- Separate OKRs from performance reviews
- Expect 60-70% achievement on aspirational OKRs (by design)
- Celebrate learning from ambitious misses
- Distinguish "committed" (must hit) from "aspirational" (stretch)
- Score on ambition + progress, not just binary hit/miss

## Waterfall OKRs

### The Pattern
Company sets OKRs → Teams told exactly what to build → Teams execute plan → Quarter ends → Repeat

This is waterfall planning with OKR labels slapped on top.

### Symptoms
- OKRs assigned top-down without team input
- KRs prescribe HOW (tasks) not WHAT (outcomes)
- No mid-quarter adjustment allowed
- Teams have zero autonomy in approach
- OKRs are just a renamed project plan

### Correct OKR Flow
```
Company: "Where do we need to go?" (Objectives)
       ↓
Teams: "How will we know we're there?" (Key Results - measurable)
       ↓
Teams: "What's the best way to get there?" (Initiatives - flexible)
```

### Mid-Quarter Adaptation
- If an approach isn't working: pivot the initiative, keep the KR
- If market changes: update KRs with documented rationale
- Regular check-ins surface "still relevant?" question

## Misalignment

### Vertical Misalignment
```
Company OKR: Expand into enterprise market
Team OKR: Improve page load speed by 50%

Problem: team OKR doesn't connect to company direction
Fix: "Enterprise trial-to-paid conversion increases from 10% to 20%"
```

### Horizontal Misalignment
```
Team A OKR: Maximize new user signups
Team B OKR: Reduce support ticket volume

Problem: A drives volume that overwhelms B
Fix: Shared OKR or explicit dependency acknowledgment
```

### Too Many OKRs
- More than 3-5 objectives = no focus
- More than 3-4 KRs per objective = diluted attention
- If everything is a priority, nothing is

### Alignment Checklist
- [ ] Each team OKR maps to at least one company OKR
- [ ] Cross-team dependencies identified and acknowledged
- [ ] No conflicting KRs between teams
- [ ] Total OKR count per team: 2-3 objectives, 2-4 KRs each

## Other Anti-Patterns

### KRs Without Baseline
"Increase customer satisfaction"  -  from what? To what? By when?
Fix: Always include current state, target state, and measurement method.

### Binary KRs
"Launch feature X"  -  either 0% or 100%, no gradient of progress.
Fix: Break into measurable milestones or reframe as outcome.

### Vanity KRs
"Reach 1M page views"  -  impressive number, no connection to value.
Fix: "Reach 1M page views with >3 min avg session duration" (engagement matters).

### Orphan OKRs
OKRs set in planning, never reviewed, rediscovered at quarter end.
Fix: Weekly check-ins (5 min), bi-weekly scoring, monthly retrospective.

### Copy-Paste OKRs
Same OKRs every quarter with slightly higher numbers.
Fix: Refresh objectives based on current strategic context. Repeating KRs with higher targets is fine IF the objective is still correct.

## Healthy OKR Indicators

- Teams can explain HOW their work connects to company objectives
- Mid-quarter pivots happen (and are celebrated, not punished)
- Some KRs are missed (targets were genuinely ambitious)
- Teams propose their own KRs (not just receive them)
- Cross-team conversations happen because of shared/dependent KRs
- OKRs are referenced in planning and prioritization discussions weekly
- Scoring happens honestly (no fudging numbers to look good)
