# Alignment & Cascade

## Principle

Company sets direction (WHAT matters). Teams choose execution path (HOW to get there).
Cascade = translate intent to local context, not copy-paste.

## Cascade Levels

```
Company Obj: "Become the market leader in SMB segment"
  -> Team Obj: "Make onboarding frictionless for SMB users"
    -> Individual KR: "Reduce SMB time-to-value from 7d to 2d"
```

## Alignment Mapping Exercise

For each team OKR, answer:
1. Which company OKR does this ladder to?
2. What's the causal link? (If we achieve X, it contributes to company Y because Z)
3. Who else is working toward the same company OKR? (dependency scan)
4. If our team OKR succeeds but company OKR fails, what's missing?

## Dependency Identification

| Question | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Does our KR require another team's output? | Document dependency, agree timeline |
| Does another team depend on us? | Surface in their planning, commit to delivery date |
| Shared metric across teams? | Split ownership: each team owns their contribution slice |
| Conflicting KRs across teams? | Escalate to leadership for priority call |

## Connection Framework

When asked "How does my work connect to company goals?":

1. **State your KR** in one sentence
2. **Name the team Objective** it belongs to
3. **Link to company Objective** with causal chain
4. **Quantify contribution**: "Our KR accounts for ~30% of the team target"

If you can't complete steps 2-3: alignment gap exists. Fix before committing.

## Bottom-Up Laddering

Doerr uses "cascading" (top-down direction) and "laddering" (bottom-up alignment). WhatMatters teaches "Four Different Ways OKRs Align"  -  not a single prescribed method.

Teams propose OKRs that:
- Address company direction in their domain
- Include locally-identified opportunities leadership may not see
- Balance assigned priorities (top-down) with team-identified gaps (bottom-up)

Ratio: ~60% top-down aligned, ~40% team-originated. Pure top-down kills ownership.

## Red Flags

- Team OKR has no connection to any company OKR
- Multiple teams have identical KRs (duplication, not collaboration)
- Team OKR contradicts another team's OKR
- Individual can't explain how their KR connects to team Objective
- 100% top-down cascade with no team input
