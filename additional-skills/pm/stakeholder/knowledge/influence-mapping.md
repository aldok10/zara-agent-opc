# Stakeholder Influence Mapping

## Power/Interest Grid

The foundational tool for stakeholder analysis. Maps each stakeholder on two axes:

```
          High Power
              │
    Manage    │    Collaborate
    Closely   │    (Key Players)
              │
 ─────────────┼─────────────── Interest
              │
    Monitor   │    Keep
    (Minimal) │    Informed
              │
          Low Power
```

### Quadrant Strategies

| Quadrant | Power | Interest | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key Players | High | High | Collaborate, co-create, involve in decisions |
| Manage Closely | High | Low | Keep satisfied, don't overwhelm with detail |
| Keep Informed | Low | High | Regular updates, listen to concerns |
| Monitor | Low | Low | Minimal effort, watch for movement |

### Mapping Process
1. List all stakeholders (people AND groups)
2. Assess power: can they block/enable the initiative?
3. Assess interest: how much do they care about the outcome?
4. Plot on grid
5. Re-assess quarterly (positions change)

## Engagement Strategy

### For Key Players (High Power, High Interest)
- Involve in design/planning decisions
- Regular 1:1 meetings (weekly or bi-weekly)
- Seek their input BEFORE announcing decisions
- Give them credit and visibility
- They become your champions if engaged well

### For Manage Closely (High Power, Low Interest)
- Don't bore with details — give executive summaries
- Highlight what matters TO THEM (their goals, their metrics)
- Proactive escalation of risks that could affect them
- Monthly touchpoints, brief and focused
- Danger: if interest increases suddenly (crisis), they become Key Players

### For Keep Informed (Low Power, High Interest)
- Consistent updates (newsletter, async docs)
- Invite to demos and showcases
- Listen — they often have domain expertise
- Warning: they may gain power (promotion, org change)

### For Monitor (Low Power, Low Interest)
- General broadcast communications only
- Don't invest significant time
- Watch for signals of increasing interest or power

## Communication Cadence

### Template by Stakeholder Type

```
Key Player: VP Engineering
- Channel: 1:1 biweekly, Slack DM for urgent
- Format: discussion, decision-making
- Content: strategy, risks, tradeoffs
- Frequency: biweekly + ad hoc

Manage Closely: CFO
- Channel: monthly exec report, email for escalation
- Format: 1-page brief, metrics, asks
- Content: budget impact, headcount, ROI
- Frequency: monthly

Keep Informed: Design team
- Channel: shared Slack channel, sprint demos
- Format: visual, show progress
- Content: what's changing, what's coming
- Frequency: weekly async, biweekly demo
```

## Influence Tactics

### Building Support
- **Reciprocity**: help them with their goals first
- **Coalition**: align with other stakeholders (strength in numbers)
- **Evidence**: data speaks louder than opinions
- **Early involvement**: people support what they help create
- **Framing**: connect your initiative to THEIR priorities

### Handling Opposition
- Understand their concerns genuinely (not just to counter them)
- Find partial alignment (agree on problem, differ on solution)
- Address risks explicitly (don't dismiss their worries)
- Private conversation before public disagreement
- Find win-win: can you adjust approach to address their concern?

### Common Blockers and Responses

| Blocker Concern | Response Strategy |
|---|---|
| "Too risky" | Show incremental approach, reversibility |
| "Not a priority" | Connect to their OKRs, show customer data |
| "Too expensive" | Show cost of inaction, phased investment |
| "We tried before" | Explain what's different now (new data, tech, market) |
| "My team is overloaded" | Offer to reduce scope, phase delivery |

## Advanced Techniques

### Stakeholder Ecosystem Map
Beyond individual mapping — understand relationships between stakeholders:
```
[CTO] ←trusts→ [VP Eng] ←reports to→ [CEO]
   ↑                                       ↑
influences                            listens to
   ↑                                       ↑
[Tech Lead] ←conflicts with→ [VP Product]
```

### Identifying Hidden Stakeholders
- Ask: "Who else should know about this?"
- Check: who approved similar initiatives before?
- Look: who owns the budget? Who owns the resources?
- Listen: who do decision-makers consult informally?

### Influence Trajectory
Track over time:
```
Quarter 1: VP Sales = Monitor (low power, low interest)
Quarter 2: VP Sales = Manage Closely (gained power over data team)
Quarter 3: VP Sales = Key Player (initiative affects revenue directly)
```

## Running a Stakeholder Workshop

### Format (60 min)
1. **List** (10 min): brainstorm all stakeholders on sticky notes
2. **Plot** (15 min): place on power/interest grid together
3. **Discuss** (20 min): challenge placement, identify gaps
4. **Plan** (15 min): assign engagement strategies and owners

### Refresh Triggers
- Org restructuring
- New leadership
- Project enters new phase
- Major pivot or scope change
- After an escalation or surprise blocker

## Anti-Patterns

- Mapping once, never updating (stakeholder dynamics shift)
- Treating all stakeholders the same (wastes effort, under-serves key players)
- Ignoring low-power stakeholders who have expertise
- Informing but never listening (communication ≠ engagement)
- Political naivety: assuming good ideas sell themselves
- Over-communicating with everyone (noise overwhelms signal)
- Not documenting stakeholder preferences (next PM starts from scratch)
