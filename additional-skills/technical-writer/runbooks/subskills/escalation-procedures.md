# Escalation Procedures

## Core Concepts
- Escalation procedures document when, how, and to whom issues should be escalated
- Clear criteria: define thresholds that trigger escalation (time elapsed, severity, blast radius)
- Contact information: names, roles, communication channels, timezone coverage
- Chain of command: who has authority to make decisions at each escalation level
- Handoff protocol: what information must be transferred during escalation

## Key Practices
- Define escalation triggers with specific, measurable criteria (not "when it seems bad")
- Include multiple contact methods per person (phone, Slack, email) with preference order
- Document what to communicate during escalation: timeline, actions taken, current hypothesis, impact
- Provide decision trees: "if X and Y, escalate to Z; if only X, escalate to W"
- Review and update contacts quarterly (people change roles, leave, change schedules)

## Common Mistakes
- Vague criteria that leave responders unsure whether to escalate
- Contact lists with outdated information (person left, role changed, wrong phone number)
- No clear ownership after escalation—both parties assume the other is handling it

## When to Apply
- Building incident response processes for a new team or service
- Onboarding new on-call engineers who need to know escalation paths
- Documenting cross-team dependencies where escalation crosses organizational boundaries
